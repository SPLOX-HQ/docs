Your plan holds two rolling windows — five hours and a week — and between them
they are the whole of what a subscription grants. The first bounds a burst; the
second bounds living at that burst indefinitely. **Usage & billing** in settings
shows how full each one is.

## What a window measures

Every call the platform pays for on your behalf — a model generating tokens, a
tool doing work — is priced when it finishes and counted against your windows.
The window is the sum of those charges inside it, as a percentage of the cap
your plan sets.

Two consequences worth knowing:

- **The unit is money, not messages.** A long turn on an expensive model moves
  the bar further than a short one on a cheap model. There is no message count
  to budget against.
- **A call on your own key does not count.** A model reached through a
  connection you added is paid by you to your provider. It shows in the usage
  graphs and nothing else — it fills no window.

The caps themselves are not published: what the product tells you instead is
the percentage. See [Plans](/account/plans) for how the tiers compare.

## The windows are fixed, not sliding

A window is not a trailing sum over the last five hours. The first call opens a
window, that window ends exactly five hours later, and at that moment the whole
allowance comes back at once. Inside a window the percentage only ever climbs.

The **Resets** line is that moment, and it means exactly one thing everywhere it
appears: when the percentage next goes down. The countdown next to it is the
same wait, computed on the server's clock, which is why the reset line on this
screen agrees with the one on a refusal.

When a window is not open at all — nothing has been spent since the last one
ended — there is no reset line. Nothing is being held against you, and the
honest line is no line.

## Reading a row

Each row is one window: its name, how full it is, the bar, and when it frees up.

| On screen | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **5-hour limit**, **Weekly limit** | which window the row is |
| **3%** | how much of the window you have used, rounded; never shown above 100% |
| the bar | the same number as a fill |
| **Resets 12:19 PM UTC · in 4h 5m** | the moment the window frees up, then the same wait as a countdown |
| **No limit** | your plan sets no cap for this window |
| **Usage unavailable** | the usage could not be read — see below |

The refresh control at the right of **Usage limits** reads the numbers again
right away rather than waiting for the next update.

## What a row can say

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A percentage below 100% | The window has room. Your work is paid for by the plan. |
| **100%** | The window is full. The plan will not pay for more work inside it. What happens next is on [Limits](/account/limits). |
| **Usage unavailable** | The usage could not be read. The row shows no numbers at all rather than 0%, because 0% would claim room nobody checked. Use the refresh control. |

The last piece of work let into a window can carry it slightly past its cap —
you get the whole cap, and nothing is held back against it in advance. The
screen still shows 100%: you are never shown more than a full window.

## What pays for the next piece of work

At any moment one of three things is true:

- **Neither window is full** — the plan pays.
- **A window is full and the wallet has money** — the wallet pays, at list
  prices. See [Extra usage](/account/extra-usage).
- **A window is full and there is nothing behind it** — the next request is
  refused until the window resets.

The usage screen and a refusal are worked out from the same numbers at the same
moment, so the two can never describe your allowance differently.

## When it frees up

Wait for the reset time, and the whole window is yours again — there is no
gradual thaw. Two other things change the picture:

- **Moving up a plan** raises the cap immediately. What you have already spent
  stays spent; it is measured against the new plan's cap, so a window that read
  100% reads a fraction of that as soon as the subscription is active.
- **Money in the wallet** does not raise the cap, but it does pay for work past
  a full window, at list prices. That is extra usage.
